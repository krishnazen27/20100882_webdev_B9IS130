SKR-GLOBAL MARINE LOGISTICS SYSTEM (MLIEMS) — PRESENTATION SPEAKER NOTES (10-15 MINUTES)
Presenter: Krishna Rao Seelam | Module: B9IS130 Web Development for Information Systems

SLIDE 1 — TITLE SLIDE:
Good morning/afternoon everyone. My name is Krishna Rao Seelam, and today I am presenting the SKR-Global Marine Logistics Import and Export Management System (MLIEMS), built for B9IS130 Web Development for Information Systems.

SLIDE 2 — PROBLEM DOMAIN:
Over 80% of global merchandise trade moves by sea, yet small and mid-sized freight forwarders still coordinate shipments using disconnected spreadsheets and emailed Bills of Lading. When customs officers and freight operators work in separate silos, a single tariff discrepancy causes expensive port demurrage delays.

SLIDE 3 — OPERATIONAL TRANSFORMATION:
SKR-Global replaces manual re-keying with centralised ISO-validated manifests, real-time customs sync, and strict role-based access control across all stakeholders.

SLIDE 4 — STAKEHOLDERS & RBAC:
The platform enforces strict separation of duties across four distinct roles: Logistics Operators who book manifests, Customs Officers who audit declarations, Clients who view only their own cargo, and Administrators who oversee system governance.

SLIDE 5 & 6 — SYSTEM ARCHITECTURE & MERN STACK:
I selected the MERN stack to maintain a single JavaScript and JSON data model end-to-end. MongoDB Atlas is particularly well suited here because a single Bill of Lading can carry a variable number of containers—embedding container sub-documents directly inside each Shipment avoids expensive SQL joins while enforcing ISO 6346 regex validation via Mongoose.

SLIDE 7 — DATA FLOW & EXTERNAL API:
Here is how a consignment flows through the system: an Operator creates a Bill of Lading, queries live destination port wind conditions via the OpenWeatherMap API, and a Customs Officer reviews that exact BoL—automatically syncing the parent shipment status upon approval or hold.

SLIDE 8 — OWASP TOP 10 SECURITY:
Hiding buttons on the front end isn't security—every API route validates a signed JWT, verifies user roles server-side, strips NoSQL injection operators using express-mongo-sanitize, hashes passwords with 12 rounds of bcrypt, and enforces rate limiting.

SLIDE 10 — CLOUD DEPLOYMENT:
Everything demonstrated is hosted live in the cloud: React on Vercel with SPA rewrite routing, Express on Render with encrypted environment variables, and MongoDB Atlas hosted in AWS Ireland.
